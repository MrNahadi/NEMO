\chapter{Conclusions and Recommendations}
\label{chap:conclusion}

\section{Chapter Overview}

This chapter states the conclusions, answers the research questions, identifies
the project contributions, and gives recommendations for validation and future
development. The conclusions remain limited to the evidence reported in the
previous chapters.

\section{Overall Conclusion}
\label{sec:overall-conclusion}

The project developed and evaluated an auditable, resource-conscious workflow
for the parameterized mass optimization of three marine structural component
archetypes. The completed system connects unit-aware engineering definitions,
first-order screening, seeded design-space exploration, bounded multi-start
Nelder--Mead search, failure-contained evaluation, traceable run records,
native Fusion CAD, semantic boundary metadata, validation packages, and a
results dashboard.

The workflow found lower-mass analytically feasible candidates for the
bracket, padeye, and stabilizer. The selected screening reductions were
72.9\%, 59.9\%, and 27.2\% relative to the configured baselines. Native Fusion
CAD mass remained within 3.8\% of the analytical mass for every baseline and
selected-finalist comparison. These results support the use of the
analytical volume models for early mass ranking and the use of the Fusion
bridge for geometry and artifact verification.

The results do not establish structural qualification. The analytical models
omit important local and service failure modes. The bracket FEA report used
1.472~N instead of the required integer input of 1472~N. Its solver values were
therefore rejected. Padeye and stabilizer manual FEA campaigns remain future
work. The finalists are screening candidates that require corrected,
mesh-converged analysis, governing design checks, verified loads and
materials, and qualified review.

The central project outcome is therefore not a claim that three components are
ready for manufacture. It is a traceable reference process that reduces a
large candidate set, generates checkable CAD, exposes missing solver evidence,
and prepares a small candidate set for disciplined engineering validation.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conclusion-rq}

\subsection{Can One Architecture Support Three Part Types?}

Yes, within the implemented scope. The registry, sampler, optimizer, logger,
bridge schema, validation-package generator, and dashboard processed all three
part identifiers. Each part supplied its own manifest, analytical evaluator,
native CAD generator, and boundary selectors through shared interfaces. This
result shows part-aware extensibility. It does not mean a fourth component can
be added without new engineering and generator code.

\subsection{Did Sampling and Multi-Start Search Find Lighter Feasible Designs?}

Yes, according to the implemented screening models. Every part completed 60
seeded Latin-hypercube evaluations and three local searches. Every selected
finalist passed the implemented factor-of-safety and displacement constraints.
Multiple starts improved the best feasible mass for all three parts compared
with the baseline start alone.

The result is not a global-optimum proof. The sample is finite,
Nelder--Mead is local, the penalties are fixed, and the bounds define the
available design family. The correct conclusion is that the method found
lower-mass feasible candidates within the declared parameterized searches.

\subsection{Did Native CAD and Boundary Identification Remain Usable?}

The bracket and padeye completed all 21 baseline-plus-sample integration
cases. The stabilizer created all 21 artifacts, although the outer test process
timed out while reading the final response. Direct artifact audit confirmed
one solid, positive volume, STEP export, and required boundary metadata for
the generated cases.

This evidence supports the generator contract over the tested vectors. It does
not prove the absence of every possible topology failure in the continuous
design space. It also does not validate the FEA boundary idealization. Human
inspection remains necessary.

\subsection{How Closely Did Analytical and CAD Mass Agree?}

The six baseline and selected-finalist comparisons differed by 0.56\% to
3.80\% in magnitude. The padeye finalist showed the largest difference. The
close agreement supports the analytical volume models for mass screening.

Mass agreement must not be extended to stress agreement. The CAD response does
not contain structural solver results. Local stress and displacement depend on
boundary conditions, contact, mesh, and three-dimensional load paths that are
not established by volume agreement.

\subsection{What Evidence Is Still Required?}

The next evidence stage must include:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item verified material and load inputs;
    \item realistic supports, fasteners, contacts, and load introduction;
    \item mesh-convergence evidence at stable monitored locations;
    \item reaction-force balance;
    \item detailed stress, displacement, and factor-of-safety results;
    \item governing failure-mode checks;
    \item applicable marine or lifting-code assessment;
    \item fatigue, buckling, corrosion, fabrication, tolerance, and inspection
    checks as relevant;
    \item uncertainty and sensitivity assessment; and
    \item proof load or other physical validation where required.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What Did the Bracket Proof of Concept Teach?}

The bracket demonstrated the intended human-reviewed chain from a
parameterized definition to a selected design, native CAD, semantic support
and load regions, and manual FEA preparation. It also exposed a serious
quality-control issue. The saved study solved successfully with a force that
was one-thousandth of the intended value.

The principal lesson is that solver completion is not acceptance. A study
checklist must compare the intended and stored load, confirm
force-per-entity behavior, inspect supports, and compare total reactions with
the applied force. The same checks must precede interpretation of future
padeye and stabilizer contours.

\section{Contribution of the Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-contribution}

\subsection{Engineering-Definition Contribution}

The project centralizes parameters, units, bounds, baselines, materials, loads,
constraints, fixed geometry, and boundary roles. This design reduces
cross-layer inconsistency and gives later work a traceable source for each
engineering input.

\subsection{Optimization-Implementation Contribution}

The project combines scaled coordinates, bound clipping, seeded LHS, three
Nelder--Mead starts, caching, failed-evaluation containment, stable logging,
and explicit feasible filtering. The stabilizer result confirms the importance
of feasible filtering when a penalty minimum remains infeasible.

\subsection{CAD-Handoff Contribution}

The project provides a versioned and inspectable exchange between an external
Python process and a Fusion add-in. Native CAD, physical properties, STEP, and
semantic boundary metadata are returned without mislabeling CAD output as FEA.

\subsection{Evidence-Management Contribution}

The report separates software, analytical, CAD, FEA, physical-validation, and
qualification evidence. It records partial status when only CAD evidence
exists. It also retains and explains a failed FEA input case. This separation
improves auditability and academic credibility.

\subsection{Practical and Educational Contribution}

The workflow was completed with a KES 0 direct-cash budget by using existing
hardware, educational Fusion access, open-source Python tools, local
computation, and digital documentation. The result provides a reusable
teaching example for model assumptions, optimization claims, desktop CAD
automation, and simulation quality control.

\section{Achievement of the Aim}
\label{sec:aim-achievement}

The aim was achieved at the workflow-development and validation-preparation
level. The project produced a working multi-part software artifact, analytical
search results, native CAD evidence, boundary metadata, validation packages,
and a preserved bracket proof of concept.

The aim was not achieved at the level of certified structural design. The
project did not complete corrected mesh-converged FEA for the three selected
finalists, physical tests, or governing-code qualification. These activities
were outside the achieved evidence level and are explicitly recommended.

\section{Recommendations}
\label{sec:recommendations}

\subsection{Immediate Bracket Validation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Recreate the bracket Static Stress study with a total downward
    \textbf{1472 N} force on the four upper sloping rib faces.
    \item Turn off force-per-entity so the four faces share the total force.
    \item Confirm the four mounting-hole cylindrical faces are the only fixed
    supports in the first idealization.
    \item Record a total vertical support reaction close to 1472~N.
    \item Use at least three systematically refined parabolic meshes.
    \item Record stress at the peak and at stable nearby monitoring regions.
    \item Compare the baseline, lightest finalist, and at least two conservative
    finalists.
    \item Add bolt bearing, fastener, weld, local plate, fatigue, corrosion,
    and fabrication checks before selecting a design.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Padeye Validation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Confirm the governing lifting standard and load combinations before
    final sizing.
    \item Apply 14\,715~N through a verified bearing region for the first
    screening study.
    \item Develop a later pin-contact model with clearance and nonlinear
    contact when the decision requires it.
    \item Check net section, bearing, shear-out, lug root, gussets, welds,
    doubler plate, and supporting structure.
    \item Include out-of-plane load, load-angle variation, fatigue, fabrication,
    inspection, and proof-load requirements.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Stabilizer Validation}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Replace the assumed pressure distribution with a load case derived
    from an accepted hydrodynamic method, CFD, model tests, or measured data.
    \item Apply positive, negative, and torsional load cases as relevant.
    \item Check root stress, tip displacement, spar and rib junctions,
    skin--spar load transfer, local panel buckling, insert termination, flange,
    and trailing edge.
    \item Include fatigue, corrosion, actuator interaction, vibration, and
    hydrodynamic uncertainty.
    \item Select shell, solid, or mixed elements based on a convergence and
    idealization study.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Software Development}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Implement the documented \code{open\_fea} contract with a real mesh
    and solver backend before exposing that mode as available.
    \item Add adaptive or feasibility-first constraint handling and compare it
    with the fixed penalty.
    \item Add sensitivity and uncertainty analysis for loads, material
    properties, concentration factors, and model coefficients.
    \item Add one request directory per CAD job or a queue before allowing
    concurrency.
    \item Record software version, Fusion version, generator version, and input
    hashes in every validation package.
    \item Add automatic comparison between the requested load and any imported
    solver report when a dependable report parser is available.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Model Calibration}

After corrected FEA results exist, compare analytical and FEA response at the
same candidate and load. If a systematic bias appears, revise the analytical
coefficients or fit a correction model. Calibration must use separate
candidates for fitting and checking. A corrected model should not be declared
valid outside the parameter and load range supported by its evidence.

\section{Recommended Validation Sequence}
\label{sec:validation-sequence}

The next project phase should follow this sequence:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item freeze the baseline and candidate parameter records;
    \item verify material and load pedigree;
    \item complete the corrected bracket FEA campaign;
    \item compare analytical and FEA trends;
    \item revise bounds or equations if the bracket model is non-conservative;
    \item complete padeye linear screening FEA;
    \item complete padeye pin-contact and code checks;
    \item derive the stabilizer hydrodynamic load cases;
    \item complete stabilizer static, buckling, and fatigue studies;
    \item select conservative finalists rather than only the lightest rows;
    \item fabricate and proof-test representative hardware where required; and
    \item obtain qualified engineering review before service use.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Research}
\label{sec:future-research}

One future direction is a formal multi-fidelity optimizer. Selected FEA results
could correct analytical predictions or train a surrogate with uncertainty.
The optimization strategy could then select new high-fidelity points where
they provide the greatest decision value.

A second direction is reliability-based design. Loads, material properties,
fabrication dimensions, corrosion loss, and model error can be represented as
uncertain quantities. Candidate selection could then use probability of
failure or reliability index instead of one deterministic factor of safety.

A third direction is multi-objective optimization. Mass can be balanced
against fabrication cost, fatigue damage, displacement, or carbon impact.
The result would be a Pareto set rather than one mass minimum.

A fourth direction is manufacturing-aware optimization. Minimum gauges, stock
sizes, tool access, bend radii, weld size, inspection access, additive
manufacturing constraints, and tolerance sensitivity can become explicit
variables or constraints.

A fifth direction is broader marine integration. The stabilizer can connect
to CFD or measured pressure data. The padeye can connect to lifting-operation
load cases. The bracket can connect to equipment vibration and foundation
stiffness. Each extension must preserve the evidence and claim boundaries used
in the current project.

\section{Final Statement}

\NEMO\ shows that a student-scale engineering workflow can automate the
repeatable parts of parametric search and CAD generation while leaving
high-consequence structural judgment visible. The strongest conclusion is not
that the lightest analytical candidates are ready for use. It is that the
workflow makes it difficult to confuse a prediction, a CAD property, a solver
setup, and a validated design.

The project should therefore be continued as a validation program. The current
repository provides the definitions, candidate history, CAD generators,
boundary roles, checklists, and proof-of-concept lessons needed for that work.
